# Editorial Styles

Research is only as powerful as the clarity with which it is communicated. While the substance of our work is found in the data, its credibility is anchored in the precision of its presentation. Consistency ensures that a reader’s attention remains fixed on the evidence rather than being distracted by the friction of shifting terminology or erratic punctuation. This handbook provides the technical architecture for BIGD’s house style—a set of shared standards designed to ensure that our prose is as rigorous as our methodology.

---

## 1. Spelling and Institutional Terminology

BIGD largely uses British English (BrE) while modernizing certain aspects to align with international research standards.

### 1.1. Spelling Protocols

| Category | Rule | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Suffixes** | Always use `-yze`,`-ize`, and `-zation` | `analyze`, `prioritize`, `randomization` |
| **British Roots** | Retain standard BrE spelling for other terms | `colour`, `centre`, `labour`, `per cent` |
| **Exceptions** | Always use the `program` spelling | `program` (never `programme`) |
| **Proper Nouns** | Do not alter official names of external entities | `Center for Global Development` |

### 1.2. Institutional Terminology

* **Mathkendro:** Use this specific spelling; do not use `Mathkendra`.
* **BRAC University:** `BRAC` must be fully capitalized; never write `Brac University`.
* **Technical Terms:** Use `randomized controlled trial`; do not use “randomized control trial.”
* **Hyphenation:** Always hyphenate `well-being`. 
    * Treat `socioeconomic` and `sociocultural` as single, unhyphenated words.

## 2. Grammar and Inclusivity

Precision in grammar and inclusivity in language reflect BIGD’s commitment to rigorous research and the dignity of the populations we study.

### 2.1. Data and Adjectives

* **Data Agreement:** Treat `data` as plural (e.g., `The data are consistent`).
* **Hyphenation of Adjectives:** Use a hyphen (`-`) for compound adjectives that precede a noun (`a well-known study`) but not when they follow the noun (`the study is well known`).
* **The “-ly” Rule:** Do not hyphenate adverbs ending in `-ly` (e.g., `a highly significant p-value`).

### 2.2. Inclusive Language

* **Singular They:** Use `they/them/their` as a generic third-person singular pronoun to maintain gender neutrality in narrative and data descriptions.
* **Person-First Language:** Focus on the individual before the condition (e.g., `person with a disability` rather than `disabled person`).
* **Capitalization:** Capitalize racial and ethnic groups including `Black`, `White`, `Indigenous`, and `Bengali`.

## 3. Capitalization and Italics

Capitalization at BIGD follows strict hierarchical rules to ensure that headings, job titles, and specific entities are distinguished from general prose.

### 3.1. Capitalization Standards

| Rule Type | Guidelines |
| :--- | :--- |
| **APA Title Case** | Use for headings and study titles. |
| **Capitalize** | First and last words; all major words; and minor words of four or more letters (`With`, `From`, `About`, `Between`, `Through`). |
| **Lowercase** | Minor words of three or fewer letters (`and`, `as`, `but`, `for`, `in`, `of`, `on`, `the`, `to`, `via`). |
| **Job Titles** | Capitalize only when preceding a name (`Executive Director Dr Imran Matin`). Lowercase when standing alone. |
| **Government** | Capitalize for specific entities (`Government of Bangladesh`); lowercase for general mentions. |

*Note.* Always capitalize the final word of a title regardless of length (e.g., `What the Research Is For`).

### 3.2. Italics

Italicize key technical terms or foreign phrases **only when first defined**; use standard text thereafter.

## 4. Punctuation

Proper usage of punctuation ensures that research findings are readable and prevents ambiguity in complex sentences.

### 4.1. Spacing and Dashes

* **Spacing:** Use exactly **one space** after a period.
* **Oxford Comma:** The serial comma is mandatory for all lists of three or more items (`research, advocacy, and impact`).
* **Dash Standards:** **En-dash (`–`):** For numerical ranges (e.g., `2024–2026`).
    * **Em-dash (`—`):** For parenthetical breaks without spaces on either side.

---

## 5. Lists and Quotations

Lists and quotations facilitate the presentation of complex qualitative and quantitative data while maintaining proper attribution.

### 5.1. List Types and Punctuation

| List Type | Format | Punctuation Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **In-sentence** | `(i)`, `(ii)`, `(iii)` | Use lowercase roman numerals in parentheses. |
| **Bulleted (Fragments)** | `•` | Start lowercase; end with comma/semicolon; period only after the final item. |
| **Bulleted (Sentences)** | `•` | Start uppercase; end with a period. |
| **Vertical Numbered** | `1.`, `2.`, `a.`, `b.` | Reserved for sequential steps or prioritized conclusions. |

### 5.2. Quotations

* **Placement:** Periods (`.`) and commas (`,`) must be placed inside quotation marks (`.”`). Semicolons (`;`) and colons (`:`) stay outside.
* **Brackets [ ]:** Use square brackets for author-inserted explanations or corrections within a quoted text (e.g., `“The [Mathkendro] centre was inaugurated in 2023.”`).
* **Block Quotes:** For quotes of `40 words or more`, indent the entire block 0.5 inches and omit quotation marks. Place the citation after the final period.
* **Contractions:** Contractions (`don’t`, `it’s`) are permitted to keep reports accessible to a broad audience.

## 6. Numerals, Currency, and Dates

Numerical data must be formatted to ensure immediate readability, particularly in dense statistical reports and policy briefs.

### 6.1. Numerals

* **Zero to Nine:** Spell out numbers one through nine in the body text.
* **10 and Above:** Use numerals for 10 and higher.
* **Exceptions:** Always use numerals for percentages, units of measurement, and exact monetary values (`5%`, `2 kg`, `BDT 9`).
* **Sentence Starts:** Never start a sentence with a numeral; spell the number out completely (`Seventy-five per cent households...`).

### 6.2. Currency and Dates

* **Currency:** Use the ISO code followed by a space and the figure (`USD 100` or `BDT 5,000`). Avoid symbols like `$` or `৳`.
* **Dates:** Use the `Day Month Year` format (`26 March 2026`).
* **Time:** Use a 12-hour clock with `AM/PM` (`10:30 AM`).

---

## 7. Statistics and Mathematics

BIGD follows the APA convention of italicizing Latin characters while keeping Greek characters upright.

### 7.1. Notation and Symbols

* **Italics:** Italicize standard statistical symbols (`p`, `t`, `F`, `M`, `SD`, `R^2`) and sample sizes (`N`, `n`).
* **Standard Type:** Greek letters (`α`, `β`, `γ`, `δ`, `ε`) must remain in roman (upright) type.
* **The Leading Zero:** Use a leading zero for values that can exceed 1 (e.g., `0.50 cm`).
    * Omit the leading zero for values that cannot exceed 1 (e.g., `p = .05`).
* **Operators:** Place a single space on both sides of operators (e.g., `n = 45`; `p < .01`).

---

## 8. Tables and Figures

Data visualizations should be clean and free of unnecessary clutter.

### 8.1. Design and Captions

* **Lines:** Omit vertical lines on the far left and right sides. Only use horizontal lines above and below the header and at the bottom of the table.
* **Labels:** Place the caption—table/figure number followed by a period, followed by the Title in Italics and Title Case on the same line (e.g., Table 1. *Socioeconomic Indicators of Respondents*)—above the respective table/figure.
* **Notes and Sources:** Place immediately below the table in a smaller font. Use *Note.* in italics for qualitative info and *Source.* in italics for attribution.

---

## 9. Acronyms and Abbreviations

### 9.1. Introduction and Style

* **First Mention:** Spell out the full name followed by the acronym in parentheses: `Government of Bangladesh (GoB)`.
* **Subsequent Use:** Use the acronym alone thereafter. Do not use periods within acronyms (`NGO`, not `N.G.O.`).
* **Pluralization:** Add a lowercase “s” without an apostrophe (`RCTs`, `NGOs`).
* **Abbreviations:** Do not include periods with abbreviated titles or common contractions like `Dr`, `PhD`, `vs`.

---

## 10. References and In-Text Citations

BIGD follows the [APA 7th Edition](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references) style for all in-text citations and references. The reference list must appear before the appendices.

---

## 11. Appendices

Appendices house supplementary materials such as survey instruments or raw data tables.

### 11.1. Labeling and Layout

* **Labeling:** Use `Appendix` (if one) or `Appendix A`, `Appendix B` (if multiple).
* **New Page:** Each appendix must begin on a new page with its own title.
* **Cross-references:** Always capitalize “Appendix” when referring to it in the body text (e.g., `“See Appendix A”`).
